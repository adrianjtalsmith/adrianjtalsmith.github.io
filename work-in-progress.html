Drafts

  • Elizan thought and generative artificial systems. Draft, September 2026. PDF Joseph Weizenbaum's secretary knew that ELIZA was a program and asked to be left alone with it all the same. If she was mistaken, it is not obvious exactly how. The paper takes her as the first of many Elizans: people who conceive of that with which they interact as a who rather than an it. Following a remark of Turing's, it fixes the target of Elizan thought, a conversational particular tracked through an exchange, in order to bring into relief the conception under which that target is regarded, and it works through the entities involved in an LLM service (model types, services, personas, instance and session agents) to ask which of them can bear that conception. Four kinds of mistake fall out: about the kind of target, about its individuation, about the continuity of its interpretation, and about whether its narration of its own conduct governs that conduct. The paper closes by asking what an interface would have to show its users for these mistakes to be avoidable.

Minds, metaphors, and machines

A programme of work with Alex Grzankowski (King's) and colleagues in user-experience research. The common question is whether the concepts through which people understand generative artificial systems are adequate to the systems themselves, and what follows for design when they are not.

  • Talk to me like a human. With Aaron Kagan. GraspingAI, August 2026. Read at GraspingAI Conversation is a familiar way of interacting with a system; no one need read a manual. But nothing about interacting in language requires that the system be addressed as an interlocutor, and doing so has a cost: users apply norms of honesty, consistency, and commitment that the system is in no position to meet. The piece sets out this dilemma and some ways of designing around it.
  • Working as intended: Narrow and wide conceptions of LLM failure. Draft; presented at the Philosophy of AI Conference 2026, School of Advanced Study, London. A model may work exactly as intended and yet fail to meet the norms of the situation in which it is deployed. The paper distinguishes narrow failures, internal to a model's operation, from wide failures of this second kind, and argues that some of what are called alignment problems are better described as cases of normative incapacity.
  • Migrated metaphors: How AI vocabulary and interfaces shape thought. With Alex Grzankowski. Draft. Terms such as 'hallucination', 'memory', and 'reasoning' have a technical use within the field, but they acquire further commitments as they pass into public use. We offer a taxonomy of the families of metaphor now in circulation, an account of this migration, and a framework for judging when a metaphor is apt for a given purpose and audience. A short entry on AI metaphors appeared in the Philosophical Glossary of AI in May 2026.
  • Generative interface design. Design research with GraspingAI. If the mistakes Elizans make are encouraged by the interface, then an interface can be designed to discourage them. This project develops a pattern library and a set of testable hypotheses for interfaces that do not imply a single, persistent interlocutor: several co-equal outputs rather than one confident answer, sources shown before their synthesis, memory that can be inspected and edited, and self-reference that is functional rather than personal.
  • Intentional Systems. With Alex Grzankowski, Aaron Kagan, David Pearl, and Allen Bevans. Funded by a King's SPARK Innovation award, 2026–27. A networked knowledge platform pairing short accounts of ideas from philosophy and psychology with their implications for the design of digital tools.

Bodily self-consciousness

  • Bodily Self-Consciousness. Monograph under contract with Routledge, New Problems in Philosophy series. Six chapters drafted. There is an impersonal way of thinking about the body that one is: it is one body amongst others. There is also a first-personal way, grounded in perception, introspection, and affect. The book argues that bodily self-consciousness runs through a single hybrid concept of the body, reached by both routes. Along the way it argues that self-location is a matter of embodied agency rather than of perception alone, that bodily awareness is a form of introspective perception, that the sense of ownership is a cognitive and affective achievement, and that no representation of the body as an integrated whole is needed to explain bodily experience.

Collaboration with GraspingAI

GraspingAI, founded by Aaron Kagan and David Pearl, is a community of philosophers, designers, and user-experience researchers thinking about AI together; it describes itself as being for thoughtful people navigating the opportunities and implications of AI. My involvement grew out of a collaboration with Aaron on anthropomorphism, artificial selfhood, and interface design, which began during his research visit to King's in 2022. That collaboration informed design prototypes tested at Google and is now the basis of a research impact case study at King's. Through GraspingAI we publish accessible pieces drawn from work in progress, such as Talk to Me Like a Human, and develop tools such as metaphorsin.ai, a dashboard that maps the metaphors used for AI in public discourse.
