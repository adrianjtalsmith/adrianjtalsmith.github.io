======================================================================
Paper:    "The Concept of Body Ownership"
Journal:  Locke Studies (special issue; editor José Luis Bermúdez)
Status:   ACCEPTED CONDITIONAL ON MINOR REVISIONS, decision dated 8 July 2026, two supportive referee
          reports, not going back to referees. Revision plan drafted 10 July 2026; revisions in hand.
          -> work-in-progress.html currently says "Paper on Locke, under review": update to
             "forthcoming in Locke Studies" (or "accepted, revisions in progress").
Abstract (from the qmd): Locke suggests a body (part) belongs to a subject just in so far as the subject
is bodily aware of it; argues for two routes (MY BODY, X'S BODY) into a single concept of body ownership,
a deflationary construal of both the fact and the sense of ownership, and a cognitive account of the sense
of ownership prompted by affective awareness of the body as a locus of immediate somatic vulnerability.

4. Bodily self-consciousness book
======================================================================
Working title:  Bodily Self-consciousness (Routledge, New Problems in Philosophy series; under contract).
Chapter 1 draft title: "Introduction: X's body is my body".
Two-sentence description (from book_outline.md and chapter drafts): The book argues that bodily
self-consciousness is channelled through a single hybrid concept of the body, reached by an impersonal
route (X'S BODY, the self as an objective biological entity) and a first-personal route (MY BODY, grounded
in perception, introspection and affect), and organised into a primitive non-conceptual layer and an
intellectual conceptual layer. Along the way it rejects purely perceptual accounts of self-location in
favour of embodied agency, defends bodily awareness as a form of introspective perception, reconceives
the sense of ownership as a cognitive-affective achievement, and denies that a representation of the body
as an integrated whole is needed to explain bodily experience.
Status: six chapters drafted (Introduction; Perspectival Self-Location; Bodily Awareness as Perception and
Introspection; Ownership; Body Representation and Embodiment; Primitive and Intellectual Bodily
Self-Consciousness), with reference apparatus and figure/table proposals; delivery to Routledge planned 2026–27.
