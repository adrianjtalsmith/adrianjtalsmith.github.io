### Bio

I am a Senior Lecturer in Philosophy at [King's College London](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/people/adrian-alsmith). My research asks how our capacities for bodily and mental activity structure our experience of the world and of ourselves. Most of my published work is on bodily self-consciousness: how the body is represented, where we locate ourselves within it, and what it is to experience a body as one's own. More recently I have been writing about the relation between perception and imagination, and about how people conceive of, talk about, and design generative AI systems.

I joined King's in 2020. Before that I held research positions at the Jean Nicod Institute (École Normale Supérieure, Paris) and the Center for Subjectivity Research at the University of Copenhagen. I took my PhD at the University of Mainz, supervised by Thomas Metzinger, after degrees at Edinburgh and Warwick.

I am an Associate Editor of *Analysis*, and co-edit the Philosophy and Cognitive Science section of the MIT Open Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science. From 2021 to 2024 I was Philosophy Programme Chair of the European Society for Philosophy and Psychology.

### Section label: RESEARCH

#### The bodily self

How does having a body of a particular structure shape what it is like to perceive and act? I have argued that we need not posit a representation of the body as an integrated whole in order to explain bodily experience and action: representations of body parts, together with the causal structure of the body itself, do the work ([*Synthese*, 2021](papers/alsmith-2021-bodily-structure-and-body-representation.pdf)). With Matthew Longo I have used pointing and perspective-taking tasks to show that people locate themselves in both head and torso, in a pattern that can be shifted experimentally ([2014](papers/alsmith-longo-2014-where-exactly-am-i.pdf), [2017](papers/alsmith-ferre-longo-2017-misalignment-paradigm.pdf)). And I defend a cognitive account of the sense of body ownership, on which experiencing a body as one's own is a matter of thinking of it as one's own, prompted by an affective awareness of the body as the locus of one's vulnerability ([2015](papers/alsmith-2015-mental-activity-and-the-sense-of-ownership.pdf); and, on Locke, [forthcoming in *Locke Studies*](publications.html)). I am writing a book, *Bodily Self-Consciousness*, under contract with Routledge, that draws these threads together.

#### Perception, imagination, and illusion

Sometimes what we perceive appears other than it is. The blanket term "illusion" hides a variety of cases, and some of them are better described as *imaginative perception*: we perceive something while imagining it to be as it is not, prompted by a prop in Kendall Walton's sense ([*Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*, 2024](papers/alsmith-2024-unmistaken-imaginative-perception-and-illusion.pdf)). This gives a way of understanding body illusions, immersive virtual environments, and, increasingly, our interactions with conversational machines.

#### How we conceive of artificial systems

Generative AI systems are presented to us through interfaces, names, and metaphors that invite us to treat them as a "who" rather than an "it". With colleagues in philosophy and in user-experience research I am studying that invitation: the *Elizan intuition* that there is someone on the other side of the screen; the vocabulary of mind ("hallucination", "reasoning", "memory") that migrates into AI discourse and carries unintended commitments with it; and the norms that a conversational interface leads users to apply, which the underlying system is structurally unable to meet. Much of this work is developed with [GraspingAI](https://www.graspingai.com/), a community of researchers, designers, and practitioners thinking about AI together; the current projects are [listed here](work-in-progress.html).

### Closing line (small, grey)

### DRAFTS

**Elizan thought and generative artificial systems.** Draft, September 2026. [PDF]
> Note: Joseph Weizenbaum's secretary asked to be left alone with ELIZA. She was not deluded: she believed she could profitably address the program the way she addressed people, and it is not obvious what, if anything, she got wrong. The paper names this stance the Elizan intuition, distinguishes three grades of vindication for it, and argues that the entity it picks out, an *artSelf*, is real but not aptly conceived on the model of a human person: it can be branched, copied, and reset in ways that frustrate the epistemic, moral, and social norms we bring to conversation.

### MINDS, METAPHORS, AND MACHINES

Section intro: A programme of work, with Alex Grzankowski (King's) and colleagues in user-experience research, on whether the conceptual tools through which people understand generative AI are adequate to the systems they are reasoning about.

**Talk to me like a human.** With Aaron Kagan. GraspingAI, August 2026. [Read at GraspingAI](https://www.graspingai.com/thinking/talk-to-me-like-a-human/)
> Note: Human-like conversation is a powerful design shortcut: nobody needs to read the manual. But it invites users to apply norms of honesty, consistency, and commitment that the system cannot meet. The piece sets out the dilemma and some ways of designing around it.

**Working as intended: Narrow and wide conceptions of LLM failure.** Draft; presented at the Philosophy of AI Conference 2026, School of Advanced Study, London.
> Note: Distinguishes failures internal to a model's operation from failures to meet the norms of the situation in which it is deployed. A system can succeed narrowly while failing widely, and some apparent alignment problems are better understood as cases of *normative incapacity*.

**Migrated metaphors: How AI vocabulary and interfaces shape thought.** With Alex Grzankowski. Draft.
> Note: A taxonomy of AI metaphor families, an account of how technical terms drawn from the vocabulary of mind acquire anthropomorphic loading as they pass into public use, and a framework for judging when a metaphor is apt for a purpose and an audience. A short entry on AI metaphors appeared in the Philosophical Glossary of AI in May 2026.

**Generative interface design.** Design research with GraspingAI.
> Note: A pattern library and a set of testable hypotheses for interfaces that do not imply a singular, persistent interlocutor: multiple co-equal outputs rather than one confident answer, provenance shown before synthesis, editable memory, and functional rather than personal self-reference.

**Intentional Systems.** With Alex Grzankowski, Aaron Kagan, David Pearl, and Allen Bevans. Funded by a King's SPARK Innovation award, 2026–27.
> Note: A networked knowledge platform pairing short accounts of ideas from philosophy and psychology with their implications for the design of digital tools.

### BODILY SELF-CONSCIOUSNESS

**Bodily Self-Consciousness.** Monograph under contract with Routledge, New Problems in Philosophy series. Six chapters drafted.
> Note: The book argues that bodily self-consciousness runs through a single hybrid concept of the body, reached by an impersonal route (the body as a biological object among others) and a first-personal route (grounded in perception, introspection, and affect). Along the way it rejects purely perceptual accounts of self-location in favour of embodied agency, defends bodily awareness as a form of introspective perception, reconceives the sense of ownership as a cognitive and affective achievement, and denies that a representation of the body as an integrated whole is needed to explain bodily experience.

**Body ownership and response-dependence.** Draft.

### COLLABORATION WITH GRASPINGAI

[GraspingAI](https://www.graspingai.com/) describes itself as a community for thoughtful people navigating the opportunities and implications of AI together. Founded by Aaron Kagan and David Pearl, it brings together philosophers, designers, and user-experience researchers, and I am one of its [people](https://www.graspingai.com/). My work with the group grew out of a long-running collaboration with Aaron, begun during his research visit to King's in 2022, on anthropomorphism, artificial selfhood, and interface design; that collaboration informed design prototypes tested at Google and now forms the basis of a research impact case study at King's. Through GraspingAI we publish accessible pieces drawn from work in progress, such as [Talk to Me Like a Human](https://www.graspingai.com/thinking/talk-to-me-like-a-human/), and develop tools including [metaphorsin.ai](https://www.metaphorsin.ai/), a dashboard mapping the metaphors used for AI in public discourse.
